For a European option this discounted expectation equals the binomial price exactly — backward induction is just this same expectation computed one step at a time.

The mathematics

No-arbitrage & the risk-neutral measure

Over one step the option payoff can be replicated by holding \(\Delta\) shares and some cash. No-arbitrage forces today's option value to equal the cost of that replicating portfolio, which turns out to be a discounted expectation under a measure \(\mathbb{Q}\) in which the stock drifts at the risk-free rate:

\[ V_0 \;=\; e^{-rT}\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[\,\text{payoff}(S_T)\,\right]. \]

CRR parameters

With \(\Delta t = T/N\), the Cox–Ross–Rubinstein choice is

\[ u = e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}, \qquad d = \frac{1}{u} = e^{-\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}, \qquad p = \frac{e^{r\Delta t} - d}{u - d}. \]

The condition \(ud = 1\) makes the tree recombine, so after \(i\) steps there are only \(i+1\) distinct prices \(S_0\,u^{\,j} d^{\,i-j}\). The probability \(p\) is chosen so the discounted stock price is a martingale: \(pu + (1-p)d = e^{r\Delta t}\).

Backward induction

Set terminal values to the payoff, then roll back:

\[ V_i^{\,j} = e^{-r\Delta t}\Big(p\,V_{i+1}^{\,j+1} + (1-p)\,V_{i+1}^{\,j}\Big). \]

American early exercise

At every node compare the continuation value above against immediate exercise, and keep the larger:

\[ V_i^{\,j} = \max\!\Big(\underbrace{\text{payoff}(S_i^{\,j})}_{\text{exercise}},\; \underbrace{e^{-r\Delta t}\big(p\,V_{i+1}^{\,j+1} + (1-p)\,V_{i+1}^{\,j}\big)}_{\text{hold}}\Big). \]

Convergence & the sawtooth

As \(N \to \infty\) the binomial price converges to Black–Scholes. The error decays like \(\mathcal{O}(1/N)\) but oscillates: whether a terminal node sits just above or below the strike \(K\) flips as \(N\) increments, shifting the payoff mass near the money and producing the characteristic even/odd sawtooth.
